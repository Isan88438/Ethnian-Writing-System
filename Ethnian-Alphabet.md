# The Ethnian Alphabet

## Overview

The Ethnian Alphabet, commonly referred to simply as Ethnian, is the alphabet used to represent languages for Ethnian readers. The Ethnian Alphabet is designed to represent pronunciation consistently across languages, with phonetics generally taking priority over conventional spelling. When a language is written in Ethnian, words are normally transcribed according to their International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) pronunciation rather than their original orthography. This allows pronunciation to remain relatively consistent regardless of the source language.

The Ethnian Alphabet is an evolving writing system and has had major changes since 2018. Its rules and inventory may be revised as the system develops.

## Phonetic Representation

Ethnian generally prioritizes pronunciation over conventional spelling. When converting a word into Ethnian, its pronunciation is analyzed and represented using Ethnian letters and digraphs.

### Digraphs and Combined Sounds

Ethnian can combine two letters to represent a single phoneme or sound. These combinations are called digraphs. Depending on the language and context, the same sequence may alternatively represent the two component sounds independently.

## Vowel Extension

Ethnian is designed to represent a wide range of vowel sounds while keeping its basic vowel inventory compact. This is done by assigning a separate letter to every possible vowel; Ethnian extends its five primary vowel letters through the use of Ethnian Vowel Diacritics.

These diacritics modify the pronunciation of a base vowel according to its height, rounding, and backness. This allows a relatively small number of symbols to represent a much larger range of vowel qualities while maintaining a consistent relationship between written forms and pronunciation.

The primary Ethnian vowel letters are **Λ**, **Є**, **I**, **O**, and **U**, representing /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, and /u/ respectively. Additional vowels are produced through diacritics and, where necessary, vowel digraphs.

### Vowel Diacritics

* **◌̷ (Height):** Changes the height of a vowel by one step. Depending on the base vowel and its assigned mapping, the vowel may be raised or lowered to represent a neighbouring vowel quality.
* * **◌̸ (Position):** Changes the frontness or backness of the vowel.
* **◌̵ (Rounding):** Reverses the rounding of the vowel, producing its rounded or unrounded counterpart where applicable.

## Treatment of Some Latin Letters

Some Latin letters represent multiple phonemes. When converting such words into Ethnian, the individual phonemes are written separately. These ensure that every written form in Ethnian directly shows its pronunciation.

| Latin Letter | IPA | Ethnian Representation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| J | /dʒ/ | DZH (D + ZH) |
| X | /ks/ | CS (C + S) |
| Q | /kw/ | CRH (C + RH) |

### Latin Letter C

The letter **C** represents /k/ in Ethnian and is functionally equivalent to the Latin letter K. Despite sharing the same glyph, Ethnian C is not equivalent to Latin C, which has no direct equivalent in the Ethnian alphabet. The Latin letter C is not native to Ethnian and has no direct equivalent; it is converted according to its pronunciation.

| C | C (anything else) | Letter | /k/ |

## Writing Direction

Ethnian can be written horizontally or vertically. Writing horizontally (left to right) is the primary way to write Ethnian and is used internationally in almost all situations; writing vertically (top to bottom) is only done on special occasions.

### Horizontal Writing
Ethnian is primarily written left to right, horizontally. Letters and digraphs appear sequentially one after the other.

### Vertical Writing
Ethnian may also be written vertically from top to bottom. When multiple columns are required, subsequent columns begin to the left of the preceding column, following the general structure of traditional East Asian vertical writing.

---

## Capitalization and Case

Ethnian has no distinction between uppercase and lowercase letters. All letters are written using their standard forms regardless of their position in a word or sentence.
